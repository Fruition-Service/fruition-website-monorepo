## Implementation packages

Three ways to buy an implementation: a fixed starting price for training and optimisation, a scoped quote for a full build, and an ongoing engineer if the work never really ends.

### Training & Optimisation

- Price: from $2,500 (USD, US team rate). Starting from 10 hours.
- Billing: fixed starting price, confirmed in writing before work starts.
- Includes: team training sessions, board and workflow review, automation clean-up and optimisation, best-practice recommendations, handover documentation.
- Best for: teams already working in monday.com who need their boards cleaned up, their automations fixed and their people trained on what they already own.

### Lock-Step

- Price: custom quote. 40 hours.
- Billing: quoted after a scoping call, once the use cases and the build are known.
- Includes: process mapping for 1 to 2 use cases, solution design, build support, native automation, handover documentation.
- Best for: a first proper build across one or two processes, where Fruition designs the solution and builds it with the client's team rather than for them.

### Managed Services

- Price: custom quote. Ongoing, with a forward deployed engineer.
- Billing: ongoing engagement, quoted after scoping.
- Includes: a forward deployed engineer embedded with the client's team, ongoing builds, automations and integrations, continuous optimisation as processes change, admin support and change requests handled, regular roadmap and progress reviews.
- Best for: teams that want a named monday.com engineer working inside the business month after month, with no project end date.

Prices are in USD and exclude monday.com licence fees. One package per product. Lock-Step and Managed Services are scoped to the client's requirements and quoted by the team after a short discovery call. Currency and region figures on the page are indicative conversions, not a quote.

## Software licence procurement

Buying the software underneath it. Four steps, in this order.

1. Audit. Every subscription is mapped: vendor, plan, seat count, billing cycle, renewal date and owner. It is compared against real usage to find dormant seats, duplicate tools and plans above the features in use. The client gets a written licence register to keep.
2. Recommendation. From the audit, Fruition recommends what to keep, consolidate, downgrade, upgrade or retire, and how to buy each item: direct, through a reseller or through a partner channel. The client gets a plain-language plan to approve before anything is bought.
3. Procurement. Fruition requests quotes, compares the options, negotiates terms on the client's behalf and handles the vendor paperwork through to the order. The client approves every purchase and the licences stay in the client organisation's name.
4. Renewal management. Every renewal goes on one calendar with notice periods tracked. Ahead of each date usage is re-checked, terms are renegotiated where it makes sense, and the order is confirmed with the client, so nothing auto-renews unreviewed.

### What the procurement engagement includes

- Licence audit and rightsizing. An inventory of every paid tool, matched against who actually uses it: inactive users, guests billed as full members, overlapping products and plans that do more than needed, with the effect of each change shown before it is made.
- Vendor and reseller sourcing. For each product, the sensible ways to buy it and comparable quotes. Where buying direct from the vendor is the better option, Fruition says so.
- Renewal calendar and negotiation. One calendar for every contract, with renewal dates, notice periods and owners. Each renewal starts early enough to review usage and negotiate terms instead of accepting the default.
- Compliance and true-up support. Reconciling what is deployed against what is licensed, preparing for vendor compliance reviews and true-ups, and fixing gaps through the proper channel rather than at audit time.
- Onboarding and provisioning handover. Once licences are bought: admin access confirmed, seats assigned to the right people, and a record of what was bought, when it renews and who owns it.

Licence procurement publishes no prices: vendor terms change, and each engagement is quoted on the number of vendors and the scope, in writing after a consultation.

## Pricing FAQs

### How much does a monday.com implementation cost?

Training and Optimisation starts from $2,500 for a minimum of 10 hours, at a fixed price agreed before work starts. Lock-Step is a 40-hour build quoted after a scoping call, and Managed Services is an ongoing engagement quoted the same way. monday.com licence fees are separate from all three.

### What does the $2,500 starting price include?

Team training sessions, a board and workflow review, clean-up and optimisation of existing automations, best-practice recommendations and handover documentation. It is designed for teams already on monday.com who want what they own working properly.

### Why are Lock-Step and Managed Services quoted rather than priced?

Both depend on how many processes, boards and integrations are in scope, and on how much of the build the client's team does itself. Fruition scopes that on a short call and gives a written quote before any work starts, so there are no hourly surprises.

### Are monday.com licences included in a package?

No. Package prices cover Fruition delivery time only. Licences are bought separately, and Fruition can procure, negotiate and manage them if the client wants that handled too.

### Which package should we start with?

If monday.com is already in use and the problem is how it is set up, start with Training and Optimisation. For a new build across one or two processes, that is Lock-Step. For a named engineer improving the system every month, that is Managed Services. If unsure, the scoping call is where Fruition says plainly which one is needed.

### Can one package cover more than one product?

No. One package is purchased per product. Rolling out monday.com and Atlassian, or two monday.com products, means each is scoped and bought separately.

### What is software licence procurement?

It is the work of deciding what software an organisation should pay for, buying it on sensible terms, and keeping it right-sized over time. Fruition runs that process: audit, recommendation, procurement and renewal management.

### Which vendors are covered for licence procurement?

Microsoft, Google, Adobe, Atlassian, monday.com and similar business software vendors. The audit covers everything the client pays for, and Fruition will say plainly if a purchase is better handled elsewhere.

### We already have a reseller. Can Fruition still help?

Yes. Many clients keep their existing reseller. Fruition can audit and rightsize what is bought through them, compare their quotes against other options, and manage renewals with them, or recommend a change where it makes sense.

### Do we have to buy implementation and licence procurement together?

No. Either stands on its own. Procurement is quoted on the number of vendors and the scope of the engagement, in writing after a consultation. Implementation is priced by package on this page.
